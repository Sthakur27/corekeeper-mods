# Runic Storage: written tier progression

2026-11-12. Proposed by the parent agent in response to Sid's request to define concepts in words before the tier artwork. These are visual directions for review, not new gameplay mechanics or user-approved final designs.

## Iteration 2 superseding feedback

Sid prefers Iron, Galaxite and Relucite, and also likes Pandorium's cleaner top. These four actual unit sprites are locked for this iteration. The other five top motifs read as arbitrary Tetris shapes: their previous glyph prescriptions below are historical, superseded by small coherent fittings with a highlight, center and shadow. Copper receives a simple inset plate/lip, Tin a neutral socket rim, Scarlet a beveled material inset, Octarine a purple/cobalt lens, and Solarite a framed gold/cyan fitting. They may read as raised or recessed at 16x16; judge visually rather than claiming precise depth. Preserve the material hierarchy and entire housing outside the central top patch. All gem pixels stay unchanged. The workbench's blue/teal feature is now rejected; its structure stays while two quieter assembly-surface alternatives are explored. A hammer remains excluded.

## Shared identity

Every unit is the same compact runic storage housing: elevated native top/front view, solid top plate, corner supports, shallow front with an inset upgrade crystal, and grounded base. Preserve the native Octarine candidate's recognizable architecture. Main/front candidates are 16x16 with hard alpha. The terminal remains the family anchor. No towers, wings, crowns, floating parts or enlarged footprints to signal rank.

The progression is from a plainly assembled metal housing to an integrated magical device. Early tiers look useful and modest; middle tiers gain engineered reinforcement and intentional rune channels; late tiers look precisely fitted, unified and luminous. Detail count must not rise endlessly: high-tier refinement comes from larger clean shapes, more integrated mounts and deliberate contrast. All tiers deserve good art, but ceremonial or luxurious treatment belongs near the end.

Use audited native material colors, not the existing gem colors or assumptions from the tier names. The same tier's gem and unit accents must share a material ramp. Neutral structural material may remain common across the family. Do not let an early tier's saturation or bright trim make it appear more advanced than later tiers.

## Tier concepts

| Tier | Concept in words | Native-size identifying feature | Progression restraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper | The starter housing: practical, visibly assembled, simple metal brackets holding a stone plate. A small crystal is secured in an ordinary socket. | Plain corner brackets; one short inset mark; unadorned crystal cup. | No bright full rim, elaborate rune or jewel-like mounting. |
| Tin | A sturdier fitted version: cleaner edging and a better-supported socket, still obviously a modest workshop-made unit. | Squared corner caps; short paired groove instead of a decorative crest. | Keep the same quiet silhouette and small focal accent as Copper. |
| Iron | A reinforced storage block: stronger dark frame and a more securely clamped crystal. It should read dependable and heavy. | One reinforced frame band; compact squared crystal clamp. | Strength rather than luxury; avoid oversized claws or glowing borders. |
| Scarlet | The first deliberately powered unit: shaped metal edging and a purposeful rune channel connect visually with its crystal. | One angular channel and small beveled shoulder accents. | First clear magical emphasis, but the plate remains mostly quiet. |
| Octarine | A precise arcane instrument: a polished inset plate, clean fitted corners and a smoothly integrated crystal socket. Preserve the liked candidate's architecture while correcting its material color. | Compact stepped rune and fitted matching corners; front crystal neatly seated. | More refined than Scarlet, without a broad brilliant rim or end-tier ornament. |
| Galaxite | A more sophisticated linked device: coordinated inset marks and paired supports make the plate and crystal feel like one assembly. | Paired stepped rune segments and balanced mounting shoulders. | Stronger unity than Octarine; no floating stars, busy sparkles or unrelated space decorations. |
| Solarite | A luminous, high-grade unit: broad clean planes and a controlled radiant inset give it an immediately premium appearance. | Split central sigil with a short bright top-edge accent; tidy metal bezel. | Reserve bright highlights for the sigil and selected edges, not the whole body. |
| Pandorium | An advanced sealed housing: interlocking corners, a deeply seated crystal and a unified rune suggest contained power and very precise construction. | Interlocking stepped corner silhouette and continuous compact rune. | Rich contrast and cleaner construction should feel above Solarite; do not merely make it darker and less legible. |
| Relucite | The finished masterwork: perfectly integrated plate, corner fittings and crystal, with the clearest, most deliberate light accents in the set. | A unified central seal and balanced bright facet/edge highlights, with a compact integrated bezel. | Highest refinement and focal clarity, not the most clutter. No early tier gets equally elaborate trim. |

## Review rules

- Treat these motifs as alternatives within the existing pixel budget, not additional layers piled onto the candidate.
- Give each unit at most one primary rune treatment and one structural accent. At 16x16, remove an accent if it damages the housing or crystal silhouette.
- The front crystal must remain visible. A top rune must not consume its contrast budget or turn the unit into a flat sign.
- Compare the whole ordered lineup at 1x and equal nearest-neighbor enlargement. Check both adjacent steps and Copper versus Relucite; later tiers should read as more refined without depending on captions.
- Match upgrade gems to their unit's verified material colors and mounting language. Preserve the previously liked gem silhouettes where possible; revise unsupported palette assignments.
- No new side/up assets, production replacement, installation or release is implied by this review pass.

## Workbench

Remove the hammer. Keep the copper-braced stone surface and crystal-setting socket with calm working space beside it. This is the shared crafting station, not another tier competing with the end-tier units. Its accent should support the terminal's established identity.
