## What to preserve across the set

- Elevated three-quarter view: visible top of hood/hat/head, shoulders, pack and item lids; face sits low beneath the brow. Compact foreshortened bodies and small feet. Avoid eye-level portraits and flat side profiles.
- Strong silhouette and large deliberate pixel clusters. The character reads first, then the profession and one or two signature props. Avoid tiny decorative clutter and random speckle.
- Layered forms: clear occlusion beneath brims, around satchels, between limbs and robes. Upper-left/top-facing planes catch warm highlights; undersides use cool, hue-tinted shadows.
- Restrained material palettes, about 3–4 principal tones per material. Earthy cloth, aged leather/wood and small brighter magical accents. Keep each NPC's own boss/theme palette; do not turn everyone into a purple/green Hemlock.
- Dark colored edges integrated with each material, softened/broken on lit sides. No uniform black contour, broad bloom, dramatic rim light or smooth gradients.
- Readable middle values against cave darkness. Reserve the brightest pixels for small glass glints, eyes or localized magic.
- Items share the same camera and lighting. Round tops read as foreshortened ovals; show thickness and a dark contact edge. Design toward a simple 16×16 icon, with distinctive silhouette at small size.
- Preserve established NPC names, species, personalities, professions and idol identities from `docs/designs/<key>.json`. Create new drawings, not recolors of vanilla sprites.

## Honest limits of the approved concept

The generated Hemlock board is a visual concept, not a validated game sprite. Its pixels have some softness and its mask remains somewhat side-on. Improve these where possible while keeping the approved appeal. Do not claim perfect pixel grids, exact palette counts, animation compatibility, true 1× scale, or in-game validation from a generated concept board.

## Native sizing and implementation standard

Updated after Sid approved and installed the size corrections on 2026-11-12. In-game verification of those corrections is still pending; publication remains on hold.

- Measure visible body pixels separately from staffs, raised tools, hats and other projecting props. Matching full silhouette height previously produced oversized bodies. Equal canvas dimensions do not imply equal rendered size.
- Inspect native sprite pixels-per-unit, pivots and the complete prefab transform chain before choosing dimensions. The four native merchant and four placed-item rigs examined here use scale (1,1,1) and 16 pixels per world unit at rest. Do not assume this for other assets. Spawn/hit squash is transient.
- Compare native, current and proposed sprites at equal pixel magnification with a shared foot baseline. Annotate body envelopes honestly as manual design judgments. Preserve intentional creature proportions: broad shells and three heads may exceed humanoid widths.
- Check every spawn item in all three forms: placed sprite, full inventory icon and small icon. Preserve native canvas/pivot conventions; compare occupied pixels, not transparent padding. Do not shrink items automatically just because their NPC needs shrinking.
- Preserve approved source cutouts and exact prompts under `Unity/Art/approved_sources/`. Convert deterministically with nearest-neighbor sampling, hard alpha, restrained palettes, point filtering and no mipmaps. Keep native animation metadata, cell containment and ground alignment; document any deliberate exceptions.
- Stage comparisons before replacing production art. After approval, apply the explicit sizing profile, validate sprite sheets, rebuild and verify actual bundle pixels, then install. In-game review precedes publication.

The reproducible size profile is [native_size_profile.json](../Unity/size_review/native_size_profile.json).
